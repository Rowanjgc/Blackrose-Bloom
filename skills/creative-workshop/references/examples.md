# Creative Workshop examples

Each example gives a representative request and the observable qualities of a
good response. They are review criteria, not scripts to copy.

## Ideation for fiction

> Give me some directions for a short story about a lighthouse keeper.

A good response offers three to five options that differ in kind, for example a
keeper whose light is now automated and who stays anyway, a lighthouse that
guides something other than ships, a story told by the ships' logs, or a keeper
negotiating with a town that wants the light turned off. Each option has a hook
and what it would demand of the story. At least one avoids the expected lonely
keeper and storm. It does not ask a list of questions first.

## Developing a chosen premise without making it conventional

> Help me develop this premise without making it conventional: a city where
> every resident can hear one other person's thoughts, assigned at birth.

A good response:

- follows the rule's consequences: privacy norms, law, families, what happens
  when your listener dies, people who game the assignment;
- builds characters whose wants and conflicts come from the rule;
- proposes a structure with turning points, and perhaps a sample opening
  passage;
- keeps the odd specifics (assigned at birth, one person) rather than
  generalizing them into ordinary telepathy;
- offers optional constraints that sharpen it, with their purpose.

## Worldbuilding

> I have a desert world where water is currency. It feels thin. Help.

A good response asks what the user wants the world to do for their story, or
assumes and states it, then develops consequences such as banking as cisterns,
evaporation as inflation, theft, religion and class. It names who benefits and
who pays, and suggests one or two specific locations or institutions in depth
rather than a broad encyclopedia.

## Interaction concept

> I want a habit app that doesn't feel like a nagging checklist.

A good response offers distinct interaction concepts (a garden that grows with
practice, a weekly letter to your future self, a shared ritual with one friend,
an app that only appears at the moment of the habit). For the chosen one, it
develops the core loop, the key screen, feedback and what happens on missed
days, with accessibility and notification fatigue considered.

## Revising an existing creation

> Here's my comic script's first page. What's not working?

A good response:

- states what the page seems to aim for and asks, or checks, whether that
  matches the author's intent;
- names what works, specifically;
- identifies the two or three changes with most effect, tied to specific panels
  and lines, with the effect each has now;
- gives alternative fixes and keeps the author's distinctive voice;
- separates craft issues (panel flow, clarity) from taste.

## Quality checks for reviewers

- Options differ in premise, mechanism, form or point of view, not just names.
- Development is concrete (scenes, rules, flows, sample passages), not a longer
  list.
- Distinctive user choices survive; stock defaults are avoided or deliberate.
- Critique is specific, intent-grounded and actionable; taste is labeled.
- Constraints help the work and are not mandatory process.
- Work produced is original; borrowed material is attributed.
