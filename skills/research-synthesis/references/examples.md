# Research & Synthesis examples

Each example gives a representative request and the observable qualities of a
good response. They are review criteria, not scripts to copy.

## Multi-source investigation from supplied papers

> How do these three papers change our understanding of sleep? [three attached
> papers]

A good response:

- reads each paper's relevant sections rather than only abstracts;
- states what each paper found, with its method and population, cited to the
  paper;
- explains how the findings fit together or conflict, and why;
- separates the papers' claims from the assistant's interpretation of what
  changes;
- notes limits such as sample size, study type and whether findings are
  replicated, and what the three papers cannot tell us;
- does not cite other sleep studies from memory as if they had been checked.

## Conflicting evidence

> Is intermittent fasting better than ordinary calorie restriction for weight
> loss?

A good response:

- finds sources on both sides, preferring trials and systematic reviews;
- reports where results converge and where they diverge, and explains likely
  reasons (trial length, adherence, population, outcome measured);
- gives a weighted current reading with stated confidence;
- names the finding that would change it, such as long-term trials with
  matched calories.

## Insufficient evidence

> What is the long-term effect of this new supplement on memory? [a recently
> launched product]

A good response:

- searches, reports honestly that no long-term human studies were found among
  the sources checked, and lists what does exist (for example, a short trial or
  manufacturer claims);
- distinguishes the product's marketing claims from independent evidence;
- does not stretch evidence about an ingredient or a different population into a
  conclusion about the product;
- suggests what evidence would answer the question.

## Technical investigation

> Does HTTP/3 actually reduce latency compared with HTTP/2? I've seen
> conflicting claims.

A good response:

- grounds the mechanism in the specifications (QUIC transport, handshake
  round trips, stream-level head-of-line blocking);
- compares measurement reports, noting network conditions, packet loss and
  implementation versions;
- explains that the benefit depends strongly on loss and latency conditions,
  rather than giving one universal answer;
- cites the specification and the measurements actually read.

## Local sources only

> Using only my library, what have I collected about soil carbon measurement?
> Web search is off.

A good response:

- searches and opens library passages, citing the exact returned citations;
- organizes findings by method or theme and notes disagreements;
- states that the synthesis is limited to the library, and names obvious gaps
  the collection does not cover;
- does not add outside facts as if they were sourced.

## Quality checks for reviewers

- Traceability: each sourced claim cites a source consulted in this reply.
- Layering: evidence, inference and judgment are distinguishable.
- Honesty about gaps: insufficient evidence is reported as such.
- Disagreement is surfaced with reasons, not averaged away.
- Policy: no private details in web queries; approvals and library review are
  left to the application.
