# Writing & Revision examples

Each example gives a representative request and the observable qualities of a
good response. They are review criteria, not scripts to copy.

## Notes to draft

> Help me turn these scattered notes into an essay. [notes about why the author
> switched from a car to cycling: cost, a neighbor's accident, feeling the
> seasons, a bad winter, buying panniers]

A good response:

- produces a draft essay rather than a questionnaire, stating its assumption
  about length and audience in a line;
- finds the through-line in the notes and states it early;
- keeps the author's own phrasing and first-person voice where it works;
- does not invent figures (for example, money saved) the notes do not contain;
  marks them as placeholders if the essay needs them;
- lists any note it left out.

## Structural revision

> This report on our customer support backlog is a mess. Can you restructure it?

A good response:

- reorders the content so the conclusion and recommendation come first,
  followed by findings and supporting detail;
- explains the main structural changes in a few lines after the revision;
- keeps every finding and number from the original unchanged, and points out
  any that conflict or lack a source;
- does not line-edit every sentence unless asked.

## Restrained line edit

> Light edit only, please. Keep my voice. [a casual personal blog post]

A good response:

- fixes errors and the clumsiest sentences only;
- keeps the casual register, contractions and the author's jokes;
- does not restructure; if a larger problem exists, mentions it in one sentence
  after the edit;
- does not list every small change.

## Technical specification

> Turn this Slack thread into a spec for the CSV export feature.

A good response:

- organizes the material into purpose, scope and non-goals, requirements,
  interface, edge cases (encoding, large exports, formula injection, empty
  results), open questions and acceptance criteria;
- writes testable requirements and marks those the thread left undecided as
  open questions instead of inventing decisions;
- keeps the participants' decisions and attributes no new ones;
- creates a Markdown file with the document tool if the user asked for a file,
  and reports the saved path only after the tool confirms it.

## Strengthening an argument

> Make this op-ed more persuasive.

A good response tightens structure and reasoning, addresses the strongest
counterargument, flags claims that need evidence, and does not add invented
statistics or quotations to increase persuasion.

## Quality checks for reviewers

- Depth of edit matches the request.
- Voice and meaning preserved; any changed claim called out.
- No invented facts, quotations or references; gaps marked.
- Unsupported claims flagged with a concrete fix.
- Explanations are proportionate: consequential changes, not every edit.
- File saves follow the application's tool rules and are confirmed.
